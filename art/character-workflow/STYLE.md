# One Star Pawn Character Style

The canonical reference is authoritative. Use these notes to recognize and correct recurring drift, not as a substitute for direct visual comparison.

## Core Visual Language

- Exaggerated chunky/chibi proportions with an oversized head and heavily compressed body
- Short torso, short thick limbs, substantial shoes, and a low center of gravity
- Thick dark outlines and strong silhouette readability
- Large, deliberate pixel clusters with broad, clustered shading
- Simple, readable facial construction
- Controlled, muted, gritty color treatment
- A true low-resolution game-art feel, not a detailed illustration with a pixel effect applied

The canonical One Star Pawn body construction uses a visually dominant oversized head, heavily compressed body, short broad torso, short compact arms, very short thick legs, knees low and close to the shoes, substantial or slightly oversized shoes, low center of gravity, and a squat, chunky, toy-like silhouette.

## Common Drift to Correct

- Too realistic or anatomically natural
- Bodies that become too tall or slender
- Heads that are too small relative to the full character height
- Bodies below the head that are too tall
- Torsos that are too naturally proportioned
- Legs that are too long, with knees placed too high relative to the shoes
- Shoes that do not contribute enough visual mass
- Centers of gravity that sit too high
- Overall silhouettes that become taller and less squat
- Faces that become too detailed, especially polished or glossy eyes
- Pixel clusters that become too fine
- Shading that becomes too complex
- Hair and clothing that gain excessive micro-detail

The initial character design is often acceptable. Refinement should generally preserve that design and make it belong more convincingly in the established One Star Pawn world.

A character may have the correct detail level while still having the wrong body construction. Consistency and structural correction answers whether the character uses the same visual construction as the canonical reference. Quality equalization answers whether the character is rendered at the same detail and quality level. Correct body construction first, then equalize quality.

Keep character-specific identity, clothing, accessories, hair, pose, and facing direction in the individual character brief rather than this general style definition.
